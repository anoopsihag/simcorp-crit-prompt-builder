Step 1 of 4 · C — Context

What's the challenge you're facing?

Be specific — include numbers, timelines, and what's at stake. The more concrete your context, the more useful your AI conversation will be.

SimCorp example
"I'm leading a cross-functional team navigating a major platform transition. We have 6 months to migrate 40% of our client base. Retention risk is high, my team is stretched, and exec visibility is increasing."
Step 2 of 4 · R — Role

What expert should AI be?

Define the persona you want AI to embody. The more specific the expertise — including industry, experience level, and perspective — the more targeted and useful the response.

SimCorp example
"Act as a senior change management consultant with 20 years of experience leading complex technology transitions at financial services and fintech firms. You understand both the technical and people sides of large-scale migrations."
Step 3 of 4 · I — Interview

Tell AI to ask you first.

This is what separates CRIT™ from generic prompts. Instructing AI to interview you — before it responds — ensures it gathers what it needs to actually help, rather than guessing. You can customise how many questions and what focus areas.

Recommended starting point
"Before you respond, ask me up to 5 clarifying questions — one at a time — to understand my situation better. Focus on constraints, stakeholders, and what success looks like for me. Only after you've asked your questions should you provide recommendations."
Step 4 of 4 · T — Task

What's the exact deliverable?

Name the specific output you want — a plan, a prioritised list, a decision framework, a draft message. Vague tasks produce vague results. The more precise you are, the more useful the output.

SimCorp example
"Give me a 90-day action plan with the 3–5 highest-leverage moves I should make, the risks I should watch for, and one question I should be asking myself that I'm probably not."
